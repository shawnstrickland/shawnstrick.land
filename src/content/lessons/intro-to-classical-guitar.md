A strong right hand technique is the foundation of everything in classical guitar. In this lesson we'll cover the essentials that will serve you for years.

## Posture and Position

Before you even play a note, your posture sets up your entire technique. Classical guitar is played with the guitar resting on the left leg (raised with a footstool or support), neck angled upward at roughly 45 degrees.

## Free Stroke vs. Rest Stroke

The two primary right hand strokes in classical guitar are:

- **Free stroke (tirando)** — the finger plucks the string and moves freely without landing on the adjacent string. Used for arpeggios and most general playing.
- **Rest stroke (apoyando)** — the finger plucks through and rests on the next string. Used for melody lines and when you want a fuller, more projected tone.

## Tone Production

The angle of your right hand nails on the string dramatically affects tone. Experiment with the nail/flesh contact ratio to find the sound you're after. More nail = brighter and more projected. More flesh = warmer and rounder.
