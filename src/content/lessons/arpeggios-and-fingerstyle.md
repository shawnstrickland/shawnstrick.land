Arpeggios are one of the most expressive tools in the fingerstyle guitarist's toolkit. This lesson covers the patterns and practice strategies that will make them second nature.

## The Core Patterns

Classical notation uses letters for right hand fingers:
- **p** (pulgar) — thumb
- **i** (indice) — index
- **m** (medio) — middle
- **a** (anular) — ring

The most fundamental pattern is **p-i-m-a**, played across four strings. Once internalized, variations flow naturally from it.

## Building Speed Without Tension

The key to fast, clean arpeggios is relaxation. Tension is the enemy. Start impossibly slow — slower than you think you need — and only increase tempo when every note rings clearly with no tension in the hand or arm.

Use a metronome. Seriously.

## Musical Application

Arpeggios aren't just exercises. Study how composers like Sor, Giuliani, and Tarrega deploy them — often as accompaniment to a melody played on the higher strings simultaneously with the thumb holding a bass line.
